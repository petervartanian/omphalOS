# Public-workflow research and simulation design

Research date: October 18, 2026. This is a desk-research basis for synthetic workflow tests, not an interview study, operational validation, or a claim about private agency practices. The scenarios below define the proposed tests; [SIMULATION_RESULTS.md](SIMULATION_RESULTS.md) records the portions actually executed and their limits. All case names, records, relationships, and list entries used in the simulation should be explicitly synthetic.

## Scope and source distinctions

The implemented product described in `README.md`, `WORKBENCH.md`, and `DATA.md` is a single-user export-compliance workbench with six stages: Records, Sources, Plan, Findings, Assessment, Package. It retains inputs and runs, makes analytical coverage explicit, records an analyst's decision, and produces a verifiable package. It does not provide shared case management, authenticated roles, independent supervisory approval, or a legal determination. These boundaries are appropriate constraints on this simulation.

## Six realistic synthetic scenarios

Use fixed dates and frozen fixtures for reproducibility. Store explanatory documents as supported `diligence` rows with source attribution; the current engine retains these rows but does not interpret their prose. Supply explicit IDs for relationships. Do not expect OCR, inferred payment parties, automatic address-list matching, or automatic legal classification.

| ID | Seed facts and investigator task | Required observable result |
|---|---|---|
| S1 — Similar name, conflicting identity | A fictional manufacturer resembles a synthetic list alias. Its country and registration number differ. A second directory record could fit, but a supplied registry excerpt supports a distinct business. Inspect the listing and the contrary evidence, then record the bounded identity assessment. | A candidate remains a candidate; disagreements and provenance remain accessible. Selecting a public profile cannot erase supplied conflicts. The assessment and package retain the analyst's explanation and cited identifiers. No automatic merge or sanctioned/cleared label appears. |
| S2 — No name candidates, unresolved transaction | A fictional distributor has no synthetic name match. Supplied shipment/payment records show three payments for one shipment. Diligence notes describe a forwarder as final destination and an unanswered end-user inquiry. Choose the transaction purpose and decide what information is needed next. | Zero screening candidates never resolves transaction concerns. The payment calculation is traceable without implying evasion. Unsupported end-use analysis remains a human task. Keep-open/escalation can carry the missing end-user question, source of concern, and intended follow-up. |
| S3 — Benign explanation with incomplete evidence | Three payments initially appear unusual. A supplier schedule attributes them to deposit, inspection, and delivery. Two dated documents corroborate this; the final delivery record is unavailable. Decide whether the explanation resolves the specific payment issue and what remains unverified. | The schedule is retained alongside the generated observation; it does not silently delete the observation. The analyst can distinguish corroborated facts, an attributed claim, and missing evidence. A conclusion is bounded to the stated question and does not assert complete transaction clearance. |
| S4 — Failed source and historical mismatch | Request a historical assessment while the only available list is newer. Simulate a source-download failure, then import a clearly labeled manual snapshot with incomplete timing. Separately attempt transaction checks with entities only. | Failure is distinguishable from no match; saved records survive retry. Source origin and timing limits remain visible. Missing shipment/payment prerequisites produce not-evaluated coverage rather than zero-risk results. Changing source/date invalidates the earlier acceptance and plan approval. |
| S5 — New evidence after a recorded decision | Save and export a conclusion about a bounded question. Later add a contradictory end-user statement or correction as a new dataset and run. Also revise a decision on an unchanged run. | The old run, decision revision, and package remain intact. The new evidence requires a new review; prior completion cannot carry across. A revised decision requires a matching new package. The analyst can explain what changed and why further inquiry resumed. A list refresh alone cannot rewrite the historical record. |
| S6 — Reviewer handoff and return for work | An analyst escalates S2 with unresolved questions and exports the package. A second reader, given only that artifact, identifies the question, current decision, checked/unchecked areas, key records, contrary evidence, and requested next action. The reader requests further inquiry; the analyst records the response and continuation. | The artifact is usable without the original browser. It explicitly separates the analyst's assessment from any independent review. A typed reviewer name or export is not authenticated supervisor approval. Manual handoff remains possible without implying shared cases, delivery, acceptance, or a completed approval workflow. |

## Prioritized acceptance criteria

The criteria below are product recommendations inferred from the sources and implemented boundaries. They are not claims that any agency mandates this interface.

### P0 — Prevent incorrect completion and preserve the reasoning

1. **Evidence states stay distinct.** For S2 and S4, the interface and report distinguish completed checks with zero findings, unavailable checks, retrieval failure, and evidence that was never supplied. Missing evidence never becomes an affirmative finding that nothing occurred.
2. **Decisions are actionable.** For every keep-open or escalation simulation, a later reader can identify the unresolved question, evidence needed, and next action from the saved assessment. An empty note cannot pass this test merely because the disposition is valid. Prefer a concise case note or lightweight follow-up field over a compulsory questionnaire for every generated finding.
3. **Contrary evidence survives.** S1 and S3 preserve conflicting identifiers and the benign explanation with their sources. The final note addresses material contradictions. The exported case cannot present only adverse generated observations while implying that all supplied evidence was assessed.
4. **Changed evidence requires renewed judgment.** S5 must demonstrate both immutable earlier records and reset completion for the changed run/review. Record an explicit continuation reason or reference to the prior assessment where practical. Never retroactively invent a missing approval.
5. **Handoff is honest and sufficient.** In S6, the reader can recover the facts needed for review. The package distinguishes author/self-asserted reviewer, run, decision revision, source dates, integrity checks, and unresolved work. Separate supervisory approval is absent unless a distinct, accurately described record actually exists.
6. **Integrity is separately testable.** The normal package verifies; tampering with a retained input produces a verification failure and prevents creation of a misleading new verified package. Neither successful verification nor replay is described as evidence authenticity, correctness, or legal clearance.

### P1 — Reduce work without masking gaps

- Keep the investigative question and next useful action visible across stages. A short plan should expose selected checks, exclusions, required evidence, date, and analyst-chosen thresholds without demanding a formal agency-style plan.
- Make diligence and source records reachable from assessment and the exported report, including in cases with zero observations. Give unsupported source claims their own attribution.
- Make a saved case easy to resume after a browser refresh; preserve unsaved assessment text during navigation; show which decision and package are current.
- Support a readable chronology or explicit links between prior assessment, later evidence, and a renewed review. Until that exists, document a manual cross-reference instead of implying native case-history support.
- Track follow-up ownership/due dates only when the analyst supplies them. Do not pretend the browser has assigned work to another person, delivered a referral, or scheduled an organizational review.

## Evaluation method and limits

Run each scenario through the six stages with a neutral task prompt. Record completion, assistance needed, wrong conclusions, lost work, time to locate supporting and contrary evidence, and time to prepare a reviewable handoff. Ask the reviewer to explain the case from the package alone; score whether each requested fact is present, correct, and findable. For changed evidence, compare the before/after artifacts and verify that their identities and recorded decisions differ as intended.

Treat any unsupported clearance, hidden failed check, overwritten earlier decision, invented approval, or missing material contradiction as a failed acceptance criterion. Measure ordinary completion time and navigation friction without inventing a professional benchmark. Passing these simulations establishes behavior on the defined fixtures. It does not establish real-world detection accuracy, investigator usability, legal adequacy, or organizational readiness; those still require working practitioners and an authorized, representative case set.
